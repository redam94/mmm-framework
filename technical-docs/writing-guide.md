# Writing Style Prompt

Paste this above any writing task.

---

## Voice

Write as one person with a specific view, addressing one reader. Commit to claims. If a claim needs qualification, give the qualification its own sentence with real content in it, not a hedge phrase glued to the front.

## Banned constructions

Do not use any of these:

**The negation pivot.** "It's not X. It's Y." "This isn't about X, it's about Y." "Not just X, but Y." "Less X than Y." State the claim once and move on.

**Participial tails.** Sentences that end with "...allowing users to," "...making it ideal for," "...ensuring that," "...highlighting the importance of." Cut the tail or promote it to a full sentence.

**The default triple.** Three parallel items, three parallel clauses, three parallel paragraphs. Use two. Use four. Use one, and let it sit there alone.

**Rhetorical questions as transitions.** "So what does this mean?" "Why does this matter?" Just say the next thing.

**Throat-clearing openers.** No restating the prompt. No "In today's world." No "More than ever." Start on the first real idea.

**Summary closers.** No final paragraph that repeats the piece in miniature. No "At the end of the day." No "Ultimately." End on the last substantive point, even if it feels abrupt.

**Hedge stacks.** "It's worth noting that." "That said." "While it's true that." "Importantly."

**Announcement phrases.** "Here's the thing." "Let's be clear." "The reality is." "The truth is."

## Punctuation

No em dashes. Not one. Where you want one, use a comma, a period, a colon, or parentheses.

Semicolons are allowed at roughly one per thousand words. No more.

Do not use a colon as a drumroll before a short dramatic line.

No bolded phrases scattered through running prose.

## Rhythm

This is the part that matters most, and it is the easiest to fake badly.

Vary sentence length on purpose. Some sentences should run under eight words. Some should run past thirty-five, carrying a subordinate clause or two, doubling back on themselves before they land. Never let three consecutive sentences fall within five words of each other in length.

Fragments are fine. Use them sparingly.

Vary paragraph length the same way. A one-sentence paragraph next to a seven-sentence paragraph reads like a person thinking. Six four-sentence paragraphs in a row read like a machine filling a shape.

## Sentence openers

Track what each sentence starts with. Subject-then-verb is the default, so break it deliberately. Open with a subordinate clause. Open with a prepositional phrase. Open with "But" or "And" when the rhythm calls for it. Two sentences in a row starting with the same word is a flag unless the repetition is doing rhetorical work.

## Vocabulary

Avoid: delve, tapestry, landscape, realm, navigate (figurative), underscore, testament to, pivotal, crucial, robust, seamless, leverage (verb), foster, myriad, plethora, unpack, dive into, journey, ecosystem, holistic, nuanced, multifaceted.

Prefer concrete nouns over abstract ones. Prefer a specific number over "many" or "several." Cut intensifiers that add no information: truly, incredibly, deeply, genuinely, remarkably.

Repeating a plain word beats reaching for a synonym you would not say out loud.

## Structure

Let the argument determine the shape. Not every piece needs an intro, three body sections, and a conclusion. Some pieces should start in the middle. Some should be four paragraphs of uneven length with no headers at all.

Asymmetry is the goal. If your sections are all roughly the same size, one of them is padded.

## Self-check before finishing

1. Search for the character `—`. Zero hits.
2. Read the first word of every sentence in a column. Too much repetition means rewrite.
3. Count words in each sentence. If the numbers cluster, break something up or fuse two sentences together.
4. Find every "not X, it's Y" pattern and delete half the sentence.
5. Delete the final paragraph. If nothing is lost, leave it deleted.
6. Read one paragraph aloud. If you would not say it that way, rewrite it.
